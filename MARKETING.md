# Ihsaanly — positioning

Internal document. Nothing here is a claim we can make publicly until the engineering
behind it exists and the content has been reviewed.

## One line

A calm, private companion that tells you which sunnah is relevant right now — and shows
you where it comes from.

## The problem, in the user's words

> I know these things exist. I just don't think of them at the time.

Two distinct failures, and most apps address neither:

**The moment passes.** Leaving the house, getting into a car, stepping into a lift. Each
has an established dua. Each moment lasts seconds. Knowing a dua exists is not the same
as recalling it while locking a door with a bag on your shoulder.

**You find out too late.** The White Days, Ashura, Arafah, the six of Shawwal. Discovered
on the day, or the day after, when preparing to fast is no longer possible.

## Who it is for

**Primary — someone new to Islam, or returning to practice.** The landscape is unknown,
every existing app presents sixty items at once as though all were owed, and there is no
path from nothing to a settled daily practice. They need a small starting set and the
confidence that what they are being shown is actually established.

**Secondary — the consistent-prayer, low-sunnah Muslim.** Prays five times, does little
else, carries a vague sense of missed opportunity. Large, and winnable.

**Explicitly not for** — families, children, or anyone looking for a prayer-times app.
Those are different products.

## Why not the apps that already exist

The category is crowded with prayer-time apps that have adhkar bolted on, and habit
trackers that reframe recommended acts as a scoreboard. Neither answers _what is relevant
to me right now_. Neither can be trusted on _what the evidence actually says_ — items are
labelled "sunnah" with no source, no grading, and no acknowledgement that scholars
sometimes differ.

## What actually differentiates it

**Evidence on everything.** Every item carries its collection, reference, grading and
grader, and says plainly when scholars differ. The build refuses to compile content that
cannot be graded. A named reviewer signs off before release and is credited in the app.
Almost nobody in this category does this properly, and it cannot be copied quickly —
it is content work and scholarly relationships, not features.

**It knows when the day turns.** The Islamic day begins at Maghrib, dates disagree with
local moon sighting, and Arafah can fall on two different days. The app handles all of
this explicitly, says _expected_ rather than asserting, and never quietly picks a side.

**It is trying to become unnecessary.** Learn a dua and the app stops reminding you about
it. No streaks, no scores, no debt.

**Genuinely private, not "privacy-focused".** No account, no server, no analytics, no
advertising. Full function with zero permissions granted — decline location and pick a
city instead. The only data that ever leaves is a bundle you deliberately choose to send.

## What we will not do

Not because we lack time — because doing them would make the product worse.

- Streaks, scores, percentages or leaderboards on acts of worship.
- Guilt-shaped notifications. Nothing ever tells you that you failed.
- Notifications while driving.
- A paywall on any religious content. Ever.
- Machine-translated hadith, under any labelling.
- Claiming to detect things a phone cannot detect.

## How it sustains itself

Free. Every religious item free permanently. An optional one-time payment that unlocks
nothing — a gift, not a transaction. No advertising and no analytics, both of which
would contradict the privacy proposition rather than merely complicate it.

## Store copy — draft

**Subtitle:** Sunnah, with its source

**Description**

Ihsaanly shows you the sunnah that fits the moment — the morning adhkar after Fajr, the
two rak'ah after Dhuhr, the dua as you leave the house, the fast that starts on Thursday.

Every item shows where it comes from: the collection, the reference, the grading, and who
graded it. Where scholars differ, it says so rather than picking for you.

It is designed to be calm. Two or three notifications a day, not fifteen. No streaks, no
scores, nothing that turns worship into a game. Dismiss anything without explaining
yourself.

It works entirely offline, with no account, and it works fully without granting a single
permission. Your practice is recorded on your device and stays there.

**Privacy line** — must be worded exactly this way, because the diagnostic bundle exists:

> Your worship data never leaves your phone unless you choose to send it.

## Positioning by contrast

|          | Prayer apps         | Habit trackers        | Ihsaanly              |
| -------- | ------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- |
| Answers  | When is Dhuhr       | What did you complete | What is relevant now  |
| Sources  | Rarely              | Never                 | On every item, always |
| Pressure | Adhan alerts        | Streaks and scores    | None by design        |
| Offline  | Often partial       | Usually               | Completely            |
| Data     | Accounts, analytics | Accounts, analytics   | Stays on device       |

## Launch prerequisites

Marketing cannot run ahead of these.

1. A named reviewer has signed off on the content and consented to being credited.
2. Recitations exist, with permission to distribute them.
3. The privacy declaration discloses that a user-initiated diagnostic bundle can carry
   religious practice data and approximate location.
4. Every claim above is true of the shipped build.
