The sunnah that fits the moment, and where it comes from.

Ihsaanly shows you what is relevant right now: the morning adhkar after sunrise, the two rak’ah after Dhuhr, the dua as you leave the house. Every item shows its source.

Coming soon to iPhone and Android

You mark the prayer. The sunnah appears.

Tap Dhuhr when you have prayed it, and the dhikr that follows is waiting. The app shows the part of the day you are in, not a clock time, so it never competes with your mosque’s timetable.

The day begins at Maghrib, so the app’s day does too.

  1. Maghrib

    Two rak’ah after the prayer.

  2. Isha

    Two rak’ah after, then Witr. Before you sleep, Ayat al-Kursi, the Quls and the dua for sleeping.

  3. Fajr

    The dua on waking, and two rak’ah before the prayer.

  4. Sunrise

    The morning adhkar, and Duha once the sun is up.

  5. Dhuhr

    Two or four rak’ah before, and two after.

  6. Asr

    The evening adhkar, until Maghrib comes round again.

After every prayer, the tasbih and Ayat al-Kursi. For the moments no clock can find, such as leaving home, setting off or going up a flight of stairs, the duas are a tap away on a home screen widget.

Every item shows its source

Each act shows where it comes from: the verse, or the hadith collection with its reference and grading, and who graded it where the collection does not carry its own. Where scholars differ, the app says so rather than choosing for you.

Every item also explains, in plain words, why it matters and how to do it.

Know what is coming

Fasting days appear in advance: Mondays and Thursdays, the White Days, Ashura, Arafah and the six of Shawwal. They are marked “expected”, because they are calculated rather than sighted.

If your community’s Hijri date differs, shift it by up to two days and everything follows.

Calm by design

Two or three reminders a day, not fifteen, and nothing during quiet hours. Dismiss anything without explaining yourself. Learn a dua and mark it as known, and the app stops reminding you about it.

Prayers and fasts you owe are kept as a simple count to make up, never a list of dates. You will not find:

  • Streaks, scores or leaderboards on acts of worship
  • A notification telling you that you failed
  • A paywall on any religious content

Questions

Why doesn’t the app show prayer times?

On purpose. It tells you which part of the day you are in, and you tell it when you have prayed. A window that is three minutes out is invisible; a clock that is three minutes out contradicts your mosque.

The Hijri date disagrees with my mosque.

Go to More, then Hijri date, and shift it by up to two days. Dates in the app are calculated, and calculation and local sighting often differ.

Why does it say “expected” next to fasting days?

Because they are calculated, not sighted. Where readings differ, such as the ninth of Dhul Hijjah locally and the day of standing in Makkah, the app shows both rather than choosing for you.

I don’t want to share my location.

You never have to. Pick your city instead, and everything works.

Why so few notifications?

Two or three a day by default, and none for the obligatory prayers unless you ask. You almost certainly have an adhan app already.

Something isn’t working.

Go to More, then Your data, then Send a diagnostic report. It shows you everything it contains before anything is sent, and you choose where it goes. Or write to support@ihsaanly.app.
